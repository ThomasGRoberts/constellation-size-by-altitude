# ESPL design implementation

- `vendor/sbi/style.css` is byte-identical to the Space-Based Interceptors reference stylesheet. `style.css` imports it and adds model-specific rules. The reference project remains unchanged.
- The full-browser white canvas, 325 px translucent floating panel, source typography, hairline sliders, large two-number statistic hierarchy, 22 px pill switches, 46 px circular camera buttons, and bottom-right linked logo with hover glint follow SBI. The view switch now sits in the panel near playback. The polyhedron project link is omitted.
- The 3D globe uses SBI's pale Earth, country borders, outlined satellite sprite texture, orbital tubes, 72-simulated-seconds-per-real-second playback, horizon control, and `setViewOffset` open-area centering. Camera distance scales with the available width on narrower screens. Thousands of sprites use camera-facing instances. All satellite sprites and orbital paths have consistent gray treatment; the 3D coverage-footprint overlay is removed. No automatic camera tracking is used.
- `sbi-map.js` adapts SBI's D3 Mollweide projection, 15,000 projected Fibonacci-site Voronoi cells, gold positive-count ramp, gray zero count, geographic boundaries, and zoom/pan. It uses the same zero-count cells for gray fill and dark contiguous-region outlines. Dashed latitude lines show the selected service-region bounds. Its compact legend puts a phrase-width gray No coverage tile beside the gradient, shows only the minimum and maximum counts, and centers the quantity between those ticks; it stays aligned with the taller panel in reserved space at viewport left. The 3D view has no legend. Voronoi geometry is cached, and playback targets a new count frame every 40 ms once the previous worker result has returned. A Web Worker computes satellite visibility while hidden 3D updates pause; dragging uses a cached map image and wheel zoom redraws after input settles.
- The chart shows fixed 1-, 10-, 50-, and 100-fold context curves and adds the exact selected fold in numeric order. Its y-axis follows the chosen optimization objective, with rounded ticks and a true vertical axis title. The CSV download includes every validated altitude and fold for the selected service region, both objectives, the optimized inclination, and explicit no-solution rows.
- `service_optimizer.py` evaluates each Walker candidate over all selectable latitude bands, sharing orbital propagation, then selects the best architecture and inclination for each band and coverage requirement. `precompute_service.py` creates complete 34-altitude × 100-fold grids for 13 service regions from ±30° through ±90°. The browser preloads them and changes the scene, statistics, chart, and chosen inclination together after validating a grid. The panel's **Methods & limits** explains regional sampling limits; the detailed search and historical Starlink comparison remain in `METHODOLOGY.md`.
